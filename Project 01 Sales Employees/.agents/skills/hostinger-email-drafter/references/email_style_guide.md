# Cold Email Style Guide — Cortech Solution
## Core Principle: Buyer-Relevant Value First

The purpose of a cold email is **NOT** to provide a website audit, deliver technical critiques, pitch generic AI, or send software agency brochures.
The email must make the prospect understand within a few seconds:

1. **What business process I noticed**
2. **What practical business improvement could be possible**
3. **Why Cortech's solution is relevant**
4. **What simple, low-friction next step they can take**

$$\text{Personalized Observation} \longrightarrow \text{Business Process} \longrightarrow \text{Potential Commercial Improvement} \longrightarrow \text{Relevant Cortech Solution} \longrightarrow \text{Low-Friction Reason to Reply} \longrightarrow \text{Signature}$$

---

## 1. Stop Over-Explaining the Problem

Do **NOT** spend most of the email describing website weaknesses or technical flaws.

### Avoid Making These the Main Hook:
- Missing H1 or header tag issues
- Minor SEO / metadata issues
- Outdated website design or styling
- Missing chatbot widget
- Static contact form by itself (without an operational bottleneck)
- Technical code quirks or page speed metrics
- Generic website criticism

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Never sell website flaws in isolation.** Only reference an observation when it directly supports a concrete, meaningful business workflow. If the observation cannot be tied to a credible operational process, **SKIP THE LEAD**.

---

## 2. Business Process Must Be Concrete

Prioritize real, high-value operational processes visible from their public website and business model:

### 1. Hotels & Hospitality:
- Direct reservations vs. third-party portal redirects
- Booking enquiries & room availability handling
- Guest communication & request routing
- OTA / direct-booking workflow & commission retention
- Housekeeping coordination & staff task management
- Check-in / check-out workflows
- Billing, invoicing & multi-property tracking

### 2. Restaurants & Food Service:
- Online ordering & WhatsApp ordering workflows
- Reservation management & table booking
- Order routing & kitchen workflow synchronization
- Menu & order management
- Delivery coordination & dispatch
- Repetitive customer enquiries & phone triage
- Invoicing / POS-related workflows

### 3. Specialty Contractors & Trades:
- Quote intake & preliminary job scoping
- Job / lead qualification before estimator dispatch
- Appointment booking & inspection scheduling
- Customer intake & after-hours enquiry handling
- Estimate preparation & follow-up workflows
- Invoice & job management workflows

### 4. Other Businesses:
- Identify the actual repetitive or high-value business process from their public website, service structure, and operating model before proposing a solution.

---

## 3. Commercial Impact Must Be Easy to Understand

Do **NOT** force complex financial formulas or percentages into every email.
Explain the potential business value in plain, practical language:

- **Reduce repetitive admin work**
- **Let the existing team handle more enquiries**
- **Reduce unnecessary coordination back-and-forth**
- **Make qualification faster**
- **Shorten the path from enquiry to booking/order**
- **Make ordering or booking easier for customers**
- **Centralize scattered, fragmented workflows**
- **Reduce manual data entry and duplicate work**
- **Allow staff to focus on higher-value work**
- **Potentially increase operational capacity without immediately adding another administrative role**

### Always Use Conditional Language for Unverified Operations:
- **BAD (Unverified internal assumption):**
  *"Your staff spends hours manually handling booking enquiries."*
- **GOOD (Observed process + conditional impact):**
  *"If availability enquiries are still being handled manually, a centralized booking workflow could reduce that repetitive back-and-forth and let the existing team handle more reservations."*

---

## 4. NEVER Invent Internal Problems (Strict Evidence Boundary)

The prospect's website shows what *customers* can do. It does **NOT** prove what happens *internally*.

### Strictly Forbidden Claims:
- *"You are losing leads."*
- *"Your staff is overwhelmed."*
- *"Your team spends 20 hours a week handling paperwork."*
- *"Customers abandon your forms."*
- *"Your employees are wasting valuable time..."*
- *"Your dispatch desk receives incomplete leads."*

Unless an exact fact is publicly documented, **NEVER** state internal friction as an established fact.

### Mandatory Conditional Framing:
- *"If..."*
- *"Could..."*
- *"May..."*
- *"If this is currently handled manually..."*
- *"A workflow like this could allow..."*

---

## 5. Do Not Automatically Pitch AI (Match Solution to Process)

**AI is a solution type, not the selling point.**
Cortech provides a full range of business software capabilities. Choose the solution that genuinely solves the operational friction:

- Custom management software
- Booking / reservation systems
- Online ordering systems
- CRM & workflow automation
- Customer & client portals
- Quote & estimation systems
- ERP systems
- Inventory management systems
- Invoice & billing systems
- Custom Next.js web applications
- E-commerce & checkout systems
- Business process automation
- AI assistants & AI agents *(Use ONLY when AI genuinely improves the workflow)*

> [!TIP]
> If a clean Next.js direct reservation tool, interactive quote calculator, or centralized workflow solves the issue, do NOT force AI into the email.

---

## 6. Sell the BUSINESS RESULT, Not the Technology

Technology is the mechanism; the business improvement is the reason to care.

- **BAD (Technology push):**
  *"I build AI agents that automate your hotel."*
- **BETTER (Business Result First):**
  *"If booking enquiries are still handled through calls and email, a centralized reservation workflow could give guests a faster way to check availability and book while reducing repetitive coordination for your team."*

Then briefly introduce the relevant Cortech capability as the enabler.

---

## 7. Make the CTA Give a Reason to Reply

Avoid lazy generic CTAs that demand a meeting or a call:
- **AVOID AS DEFAULT:**
  - *"Would you have 10 minutes this week?"*
  - *"Open to a 5-minute demo?"*
  - *"Can we schedule a call?"*
  - *"Interested in learning more?"*

Prefer a useful, low-friction next step where Cortech offers value upfront:
- *"I can send over a simple example of how this could work for your property if useful."*
- *"I can map the workflow around your current setup if you'd like to see it."*
- *"I can put together a quick example around your current ordering process."*
- *"I can show you what this could look like before you decide whether it's worth discussing."*

The CTA makes replying easy and risk-free without pressuring them for a calendar meeting immediately.

---

## 8. Keep Emails Highly Personalized (Commercial Facts)

Ground every email in **2 to 3 genuinely relevant business facts**:
- Specific business type & operating model
- Physical location / service territory
- Specific service offerings
- Observable booking, ordering, or quote process
- Multi-location footprint (if publicly available)
- Specific customer workflow visible on their website

Do **NOT** use generic compliments (*"great to see your 25 years in business"* or *"your 4.8 rating is impressive"*) merely to check a personalization box.

---

## 9. Email Structure & Word Count (80–110 Words)

Emails must be crisp, scannable, and founder-to-founder.
**Target word count: 80 to 110 words** (absolute maximum 130 words).

### The Required 6-Part Flow:
1. **Greeting**: `Hi [First Name],` (or `Hi [Company Name] team,` if no named contact).
2. **Personalized Observation**: Concrete, verifiable fact about their business and public setup.
3. **Business Process**: The specific operational process noticed on their site.
4. **Potential Commercial / Operational Improvement**: The practical benefit (reducing admin back-and-forth, increasing capacity, faster turnaround) framed conditionally.
5. **Relevant Cortech Solution**: The specific software, booking engine, or intake tool introduced naturally.
6. **Low-Friction Reason to Reply**: A zero-pressure CTA offering an example or workflow map.
7. **Canonical Signature**:
   ```text
   Best regards,

   Syed Mesum Ali Shah
   Cortech Solution
   mesum@cortechsolution.com | cortechsolution.com
   ```

---

## 10. The Strong Owner Test

Before finalizing any draft, ask:

$$\textbf{"If I were the owner reading this between other emails, would I immediately understand:}$$
1. **What process you noticed?**
2. **Why improving it could matter to my business?**
3. **What you are offering?**
4. **And why replying could be worthwhile?"**

If the answer to any of these is **NO**, rewrite or discard the draft.

---

## 11. Important: Do Not Force an Email (Skip Rules)

If the prospect does not have:
- A meaningful, observable business process,
- A credible operational improvement opportunity,
- Sufficient evidence on their public website,
- And a clear, directly relevant Cortech solution,

$$\textbf{SKIP THE LEAD.}$$
**Quality and buyer relevance are far more important than filling the queue.**

---

## 12. Final 13-Point QA Checklist

Every email must pass all checks before being written to `draft.md` or `draft_queue.json`:
1. **No fabricated internal workflow** (strictly conditional phrasing for internal operations).
2. **No fabricated financial numbers** (no fake ROI, no fake staff hours, no fake revenue percentages).
3. **No unsupported claims about lost customers or revenue**.
4. **No generic website criticism** (no selling missing H1s, minor SEO, or outdated styling).
5. **No forced AI pitch** (AI only when it genuinely fits; standard software otherwise).
6. **One primary business opportunity** (no multi-service laundry lists).
7. **Clear business impact** (in plain, understandable language).
8. **Relevant Cortech solution** (directly matched to the process).
9. **Personalized opening** (grounded in 2–3 concrete business/trade facts).
10. **CTA provides a reason to reply** (value-added next step, no pushy demo request).
11. **80–110 words** (tight, scannable, maximum 130 words).
12. **Canonical signature** (exact founder name and email).
13. **Natural human founder-to-founder tone** (plain text only, no bold/italics/emojis).
